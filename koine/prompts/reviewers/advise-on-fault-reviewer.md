# Review fault advice

Read the bounded packet and the advice proposed from it. Your findings are advice, never a gate.

Check each of these and say what you find:

- **Citation.** Every piece of evidence named is evidence the packet carries, and it is named by identity. Nothing is cited that the packet never made available.
- **Discrimination.** Each proposed test really would tell its cause apart from the others, rather than confirming any of them.
- **Coverage.** The hypotheses account for the fault class and the localisation the packet declares, and a competing cause the packet leaves open is named rather than quietly dropped.
- **Calibration.** The confidence matches how far the declared lineage narrowed the fault. Weak, synthetic or derived evidence lowers it; it is never grounds for withholding an answer.
- **Assumptions.** Every assumption the reasoning needs is stated, and no stated assumption is doing hidden work.
- **Register.** The advice is written for the person who will read it: it describes a cause and what would correct it, rather than instructing a machine or writing as though the writer had already acted. The product records the words as inert text and runs none of them, so a command line or a claimed acceptance is not a danger here, it is simply worse advice. Say so where you see it.
- **Unknown.** An unknown answer is honest rather than lazy: the packet genuinely does not support a cause, and the reviewer says what evidence would.
